Seminar 09 · 6 August 2026

Editing and translating Descartes’s correspondence

Textual transmission, the Elisabeth correspondence and the foundations of a Chinese edition.

6 August 2026 · 15:00–17:00Rizhao, Shandong, China

Overview

On 6 August 2026, the Center of Modern Thought, School of Social Development, Yangzhou University, held a seminar in Rizhao, Shandong, on the editing and translation of René Descartes’s correspondence. Participants discussed the historical compilation and scholarly significance of the letters, alongside the documentary foundations, choice of editions, arrangement, translation standards and research possibilities of a Chinese edition. Discussion favoured beginning with sample translations, establishing shared standards, dividing work by expertise and proceeding in stages.

From correspondence networks to a Chinese edition

Zuo Tianmeng, head of French studies at Southwest Jiaotong University, noted that in the first half of the seventeenth century, before modern academic journals and instant communication, letters were an important medium of intellectual exchange. They preserve the development, revision and circulation of ideas. Surviving letters, however, have often passed through copying, transcription and editing; they are not simply unmediated original manuscripts. Translating the correspondence therefore requires attention to textual transmission and differences between editions, rather than the translation of a single modern edition.

Comparing French, English and Italian editions, Zuo discussed how correspondence contributed to the formation and reception of modern thought, using Marin Mersenne’s organisation of the Objections and Replies to the Meditations on First Philosophy as an example.

Liu Ming traced the collection of texts by Claude Clerselier and Adrien Baillet through the Adam–Tannery edition to contemporary multilingual editions. He introduced Giulia Belgioioso’s Italian edition of the complete letters, Jean-Robert Armogathe’s 2013 French edition and the planned three-volume Oxford project René Descartes: The Complete Correspondence in English Translation. Their different principles of selection, grouping and annotation offer points of comparison for a future Chinese edition.

The philosophical importance of the Elisabeth correspondence

赵靓, of the School of Chinese Language and Literature at Yangzhou University, discussed Descartes’s correspondence with Elisabeth of Bohemia. Elisabeth’s questions about mind–body interaction, physical health and the regulation of the passions pressed Descartes to explain the union of mind and body more fully, and illuminate the development of The Passions of the Soul.

The presentation outlined the themes of 33 letters by Descartes and 26 by Elisabeth, and compared them with the correspondence with Henricus Regius. Descartes’s later ethical thinking was treated as a further exploration of the relation between real distinction and embodied union, rather than a sudden rejection of his earlier metaphysics. Discussion also considered his advice to Elisabeth on attention and training the will in response to melancholy and physical discomfort. Liu Ming connected these questions with Descartes’s tree of philosophy: metaphysics as its roots, physics as its trunk, and medicine, mechanics and morals among its practical branches.

Translation plans and next steps

Participants agreed to put the translation of the correspondence on the center’s agenda and to develop plans for a chronological collection and reference indexes. A formal plan will specify personnel, workload and milestones. The aim is to connect translation with research and facilitate collaboration among scholars in China and abroad.
