For authors

Submissions

Author guidance and editorial contact for Modern Philosophy.

Contact the editorial office

Modern Philosophy welcomes research on Western early modern and modern philosophy and related fields, together with scholarly translations.

modernphi24@163.com

Please include the manuscript title, the author’s name, research topic, manuscript and contact information. For current thematic plans, submissions and permissions, contact the editorial office.

Preparing a submission

This page summarises the guidance printed in the first issue, on pages 297–298 and 300–302.

  • Include the article title and its English translation, the author’s name, nationality and institution, the main text and footnotes.
  • Research articles should include Chinese and English abstracts and three to five keywords in each language. The Chinese guidelines set out abstract lengths and formatting requirements.
  • Provide the author’s institution, degree, correspondence address, postcode and email address on the first page.
  • A French abstract and keywords may also be supplied. The first-issue guidance states that, if absent, the editorial office will prepare these after acceptance from the Chinese and English material.

Complete conformity with the publication’s citation style is not required at the initial submission stage; the editorial office will request formatting changes if the manuscript is provisionally accepted.

References and scholarly standards

The publication uses footnotes. Check quotations, page numbers and editions, identify secondary citations, and retain original-language bibliographic information for foreign-language sources. Give full details on first citation and abbreviated references thereafter; credit translators and identify the volume or journal containing a contribution.

Submissions should respect intellectual property and identify sources accurately. Do not submit the same manuscript simultaneously elsewhere. Explain any related publication or permission arrangements to the editorial office.

The first-issue guidelines describe a combination of internal editorial review and external blind peer review. Permissions, republication and final publication arrangements should be confirmed with the editorial office.

Thematic enquiries

The call printed on page 299 of Issue 1 concerns “Robert Boyle and Early Modern Experimental Philosophy”, including natural philosophy, experimental method, mechanical philosophy and theology. Contact the editors for current arrangements.
